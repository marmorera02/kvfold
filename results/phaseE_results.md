# Phase E results: Wikipedia-register prose control

Per `phaseE_predictions.md`. Full data: `results/phaseE_run.json`, plus per-stage artifacts (`e2_scores_seedwiki{A,B}.json`, `e2_delta_seedwiki{A,B}.json`, `e2_assumptions_seedwiki{A,B}.json`, `e2_nli_v2_seedwiki{A,B}.json`).

## P-J (zero-contradiction precision): HOLDS

wikiB (reinforced fact ×3, zero contradictions) produced **zero** false contradictions under the same delta-routed-NLI methodology that produced 2 false positives on the legal corpus's equivalent seed 3 and 1 on seed 4. This is a genuine, positive result on the precision axis: moving off a small-template-cycling corpus onto lexically varied, human-written prose removes the specific failure mechanism Task 2 diagnosed (near-duplicate same-template clauses, differing only in incidental numbers, being confused for genuine disagreements) — there's no small template pool here for anything to duplicate.

## P-K (recall): FAILS

wikiA's planted contradiction (clause 85: "the Eiffel Tower's overall height was revised upward to 345 metres") is missed entirely — not merely misrouted; it never clears the NLI bar even when checked directly. Two independent, compounding causes, inspected directly from the data (not inferred):

1. **Routing miss.** Clause 85's section (section 9, clauses 81-90) has z_delta_L2 = **+0.632** — one of the *highest* (least topically-saturated) of the 10 sections, not the lowest. The candidate (bottom-15th-percentile) sections were 4 and 8 (z_delta_L2 = -1.15 and -1.18). Section 8 is, notably, exactly where the reinforced fact's *own* third restatement (clause 75) sits — consistent with the topical-priming mechanism still operating on the frame's own vocabulary. But the contradiction, phrased with substantial new content of its own (antenna replacement, 2022 project, public records), does not inherit that priming the way the legal corpus's N2 did (N2 was written to reuse the frame's exact vocabulary — "liability," "damages," "caps" — deliberately). At L0 and L1, clause 85's z_delta is likewise positive (+1.00, +1.11) — this isn't an L2-specific routing artifact; the contradiction doesn't read as "topically saturated" at any pyramid level on this corpus.

2. **NLI itself does not recognize the contradiction, independent of routing.** Extraction and the referent guard both worked correctly — the frame's assumption (clause 75: "...reaches a total height of 330 metres...") is present in the assumption set and correctly clears clause 85's referent guard (shared "height"/"metres"/tower reference). But the raw NLI score for this specific premise-hypothesis pair is **0.007** — and the maximum across *all* 13 guard-passed premises checked against clause 85 is **0.024**, nowhere near the 0.9 bar. This is a real limitation of the general-purpose NLI model (`MoritzLaurer/DeBERTa-v3-base-mnli-fever-anli`) on this specific kind of contradiction, not a guard or extraction failure.

## An honest note on a possible confound in the needle's own phrasing

The legal corpus's contradiction (N2) was written as an explicit, syntactically unambiguous override: *"Notwithstanding... the limitation of liability set forth in Clause 8... shall be unlimited."* Phase E's contradiction was written in a softer, update-style register: *"engineers confirmed that the... height had been revised upward to 345 metres, though the change has not yet been reflected in most public records."* Read carefully, this is phraseable as a coherent **timeline** ("it was 330m, then later revised to 345m") rather than a same-moment logical negation — and general-purpose NLI models are typically trained to *not* flag temporally-sequenced updates as contradictions, since two claims about different points in time can both be true. It's possible the near-zero NLI score reflects a defensible reading of the sentence as written, not a pure model failure — a difference in how directly the two corpora's contradictions assert logical incompatibility, not only a difference in prose register. This is flagged plainly as an alternative explanation worth knowing about, not adjudicated: per standing rule, the needle was not rewritten and rerun after seeing this result, since that would be tuning the test to produce a preferred outcome. A future phase wanting to isolate "register" from "explicitness of the override" cleanly would need a same-moment-framed contradiction on real prose as a follow-up, out of scope here.

## Gate verdict

Per the pre-registered gate: **P-K fails → the routing/extraction mechanism itself does not transfer to non-templated prose**, a stronger negative result than Task 2's (which located and half-caught its contradictions; here, neither routing nor raw NLI engages with the one planted contradiction at all). Combined with P-J's genuine hold, the net picture is coherent and worth stating precisely: **real prose fixes the precision problem (no more same-template false positives) but does not, on its own, fix or even preserve the recall problem** — if anything, it removes the (artifactual) mechanism that gave the legal corpus's Δ-routing its apparent recall success in the first place. Both halves point the same direction as the rest of the program's findings: Δ was never a real contradiction signal (P-F, refuted in Phase D); its apparent locating power on the legal corpus was riding on that corpus's own artificial vocabulary-repetition density, which genuine human-written prose does not reproduce.

## Net for the program

Phase E closes the last open question from `WRITEUP_SKELETON.md` §5's limitations (whether the locator/router framing transfers to lexically varied text). It does not reopen the detection track: recall fails outright on real prose, by a wider margin and for more fundamental reasons (the routing signal points the wrong direction; the NLI model doesn't recognize the contradiction pattern even unguarded) than the legal corpus's partial recall failure. Task 2 and E7 already closed the detection track on the templated corpus; Phase E confirms the underlying mechanism (Δ as locator) does not have a real-prose case to fall back on either.
